# Scenario Hints

Use these hints one at a time. Stop as soon as your group can continue designing.

## Urban Bean Coffee Roasters

??? tip "Hint 1: Separate the caller journeys"

    Start by separating the storefront, wholesale service, mobile employees, warehouse announcements, and after-hours experiences. Do not design one large route for every need.

??? tip "Hint 2: Focus on the wholesale caller"

    Wholesale callers need to wait for the right team, hear useful treatment, request a callback, and reach an owned destination if the team is unavailable. Ask whether a basic Hunt Group can provide all of those capabilities.

??? tip "Hint 3: Connect the desktop workflow to the CRM"

    Customer Assist screen pop is a desktop workflow for coordinators. Think about how known, duplicate, shared, and unknown phone numbers should be handled instead of assuming every number matches one CRM record.

??? tip "Hint 4: Protect business identity and ownership"

    Employees should be reachable away from their desks without exposing personal numbers. Also consider which number appears on wholesale callbacks, who owns unanswered messages, and how warehouse delivery announcements reach only approved phones.

??? tip "Hint 5: Test the exceptions"

    Walk through a holiday, weather closure, full or unavailable wholesale team, unanswered call, and warehouse announcement. Identify who activates each exception and who owns the final destination.

## Riverside Unified School District

??? tip "Hint 1: Design two levels of front doors"

    Callers may enter through the district number or directly through one of the three school numbers. Each entry point should provide a clear, school-appropriate path.

??? tip "Hint 2: Separate directory access from call handling"

    Dial by name and Dial by extension help callers find people. They do not determine whether a teacher is available during instruction or where an unanswered call should go.

??? tip "Hint 3: Match the feature to the office model"

    The school offices have three assistants, but there is no queue sign-in, callback service, or dedicated supervisor. Choose a predictable ringing and final-destination design that fits that operating model.

??? tip "Hint 4: Keep messages owned by the school"

    Teacher voicemail, school main-office voicemail, and attendance voicemail have different owners. Decide who can access each mailbox and where email notifications or audio copies should be delivered.

??? tip "Hint 5: Plan language and schedule changes"

    Think about how English and Spanish callers reach the same destinations. Then design separate behavior for school days, after hours, holidays, delayed openings, and closures, including who can activate and restore an exception.

## Questions to Ask When Your Group Is Stuck

- What should the caller experience normally?
- What happens when the preferred person or team is unavailable?
- Which feature provides the required capability, not just a similar-sounding feature?
- Who owns the final destination, mailbox, or exception?
- What assumption could change the design?
